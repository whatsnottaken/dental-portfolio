Professional Profile

Dedicated to clinical precision & patient comfort.

Dr Ashleigh Toh is a dental surgeon who completed her Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) at the National University of Singapore, followed by advanced postgraduate academic training leading to the Master of Science (MSc) in Oral Medicine from London.

Oral Medicine is the dental discipline dedicated to the non-surgical diagnosis, assessment, and management of medically related disorders affecting the oral and maxillofacial lining. These include persistent oral ulcers, chronic red and white patches, lichenoid reactions, burning mouth sensations, and salivary gland disorders.

"Careful listening, detailed tissue examination, and close collaboration with referring dental colleagues are the foundation of supportive, effective Oral Medicine care."

Recognising that oral mucosal changes often cause anxiety or prolonged discomfort, Dr Toh emphasises a calm, step-by-step diagnostic process and clear communication, ensuring each patient and their referring clinician receive a thorough, evidence-informed appraisal.

Comprehensive Diagnostic Workup

Detailed evaluation of symptom history, medication review, and thorough soft-tissue assessment to understand the underlying presentation.

Collegiate Collaboration

Prompt feedback and detailed clinical summaries back to referring dentists to ensure cohesive, ongoing patient care.

Evidence-Informed Care

Non-surgical therapeutic strategies, patient reassurance, and surveillance plans aligned with current clinical evidence.

Clinical Indications

Reasons to consider an Oral Medicine assessment

Dental practitioners may consider referral or case discussion for presentations including, but not limited to:

Persistent or Recurrent Ulceration

Ulcerations persisting longer than 2–3 weeks, frequent aphthous outbreaks, or atypical mucosal lesions warranting further evaluation.

White, Red & Mixed Patches

Leukoplakia, erythroplakia, speckled mucosal changes, or friction-independent hyperkeratosis requiring surveillance or biopsy planning.

Oral Lichen Planus & Lichenoid Reactions

Symptomatic reticular, erosive, or desquamative gingival manifestations, as well as suspected drug-induced mucosal reactions.

Burning Mouth & Dysesthesia

Chronic unexplained burning or altered oral sensations where conventional dental and periodontal sources have been excluded.

Dry Mouth & Salivary Hypofunction

Severe xerostomia, medication-induced salivary hypofunction, or clinical suspicion of systemic connective tissue disease (e.g. Sjögren's).

Unexplained Halitosis

Persistent halitosis unresolved by standard oral prophylaxis and periodontal treatment, requiring systematic assessment.

Patient Education

Understanding oral mucosal conditions

Changes in the mouth lining are common and can have many causes. The overviews below are for general educational awareness.

Mouth Ulcers

Mouth ulcers are breaks in the delicate oral lining. While common aphthous ulcers often heal within 10 to 14 days, recurrent or persistent sores can stem from nutritional factors, autoimmune processes, stress, or other health conditions.

When to seek assessment

Any ulcer that does not heal after 2 to 3 weeks, is unusually large or painful, or occurs with systemic unwellness warrants clinical examination.

Oral Lichen Planus

An inflammatory condition affecting the mucous membranes inside the mouth. It frequently appears as fine white lace-like patterns (Wickham striae), red patches, or tender erosions on the inside of the cheeks, tongue, or gums.

When to seek assessment

Seek review if you notice persistent soreness with spicy or citrus foods, bleeding red gums (desquamative gingivitis), or rough white patches.

Burning Mouth Sensation

A chronic sensation of burning, scalding, or numbness affecting the tongue, lips, roof of the mouth, or widespread oral areas. The tissues often look completely normal despite significant discomfort.

When to seek assessment

A structured evaluation helps identify possible contributing factors such as dry mouth, nutritional deficiencies, or nerve-related sensitivity.

Dry Mouth (Xerostomia)

Reduced saliva flow or a constant subjective feeling of mouth dryness. Common causes include prescription medications, salivary gland changes, or autoimmune conditions. Saliva protects teeth and gums, making assessment valuable.

When to seek assessment

If dryness causes difficulty swallowing, speaking, a sticky sensation, or rapid tooth decay. Do not discontinue prescribed medications without medical advice.

Red & White Patches

Changes in mucosal color or texture that cannot be rubbed away. While many are benign reactions to dental friction, some require close clinical monitoring, biopsy, or regular surveillance to ensure mucosal health.

When to seek assessment

Any solitary red, white, or mixed lesion persisting longer than 2 weeks without an obvious physical cause should be evaluated professionally.

Persistent Bad Breath

Ongoing oral malodor is a common and treatable concern. While frequently linked to microbial buildup on the tongue or gums, persistent cases may involve salivary issues, nasal passage secretions, or systemic factors.

When to seek assessment

When bad breath persists despite meticulous home brushing, flossing, and regular dental cleanings, a targeted assessment can identify underlying oral causes.

Important Clinical Information Notice

The information provided on this website is for general educational purposes only and is not a substitute for an in-person clinical examination, professional diagnosis, or individual medical advice. Any persistent oral symptom, sore, or colour change lasting longer than two weeks should be evaluated by a dental surgeon or doctor. For acute breathing difficulties, severe facial swelling, or rapid bleeding, please visit an emergency department immediately.

Patient Experience

What to expect during your assessment

An Oral Medicine consultation is focused on understanding your individual symptoms in depth within a calm and unhurried setting.

1

Detailed History & Discussion

We begin by discussing when your symptoms first appeared, how they fluctuate, what brings relief or aggravation, and your complete medical and medication history.

2

Thorough Soft-Tissue Examination

A gentle, systematic inspection of the entire oral lining, including the lips, cheeks, tongue borders, palate, floor of mouth, and salivary glands.

3

Diagnosis & Investigation Plan

We discuss likely causes and whether any supportive investigations—such as a small diagnostic biopsy, blood test, or salivary flow assessment—are clinically indicated.

4

Personalised Care & Review

You will receive clear guidance on symptom relief, oral care adjustments, lifestyle measures, and a tailored monitoring schedule.

Preparing for Your Visit

To help us make the most of your consultation, please bring along:

  • Current medication list, including prescribed drugs, over-the-counter supplements, and herbal products.
  • Referral letter or clinical notes from your referring dentist or doctor (if provided).
  • Previous biopsy reports, blood test results, or dental imaging relevant to your current concern.
  • Notes on your symptom timeline, including how long the condition has been present and any home remedies tried.
